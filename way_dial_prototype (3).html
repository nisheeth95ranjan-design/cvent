What moves, and what does not

The city

Night ShiftThe 1:30 to 4:30 am window when the Earth heals itself and slides the surface of the city.
MoonThe city moves with the Moon: the pull is strongest around a full Moon and weakest around a new one. It says that the city moves, not where each place lands.
Centre HillThe raised middle of the city that every ring is measured from.
RingA circle around the Centre Hill, numbered 1 (closest) to 4 (the outer edge).
NotchA direction slice like an hour on a clock, numbered 1 to 8 clockwise with 1 at the top.
CellOne ring and one notch together, for example ring 2, notch 3. There are 32 cells.
Surface layerThe top of the city: homes, shops, pharmacies and stalls. This is what slides at night.
BedrockThe deep layer that never moves. Anchors, data centres, tram rails and the cell beacons sit on it.
AnchorAn old landmark that never moves: the Banyan, the Old Tank and the Step-well.
Data centreA huge, warm, humming building that never moves. Three were dropped at random cells when the city began.
CanalA water channel the Earth opened between rings 2 and 3, along notches 7, 8, 1, 2 and 3. You can only cross at the bridge or by ferry.
BridgeA crossing over the canal. It can slide with the surface, so its notch can change.
AftershockA small extra shift after the Night Shift. The Register can be out of date for about 20 minutes.
Nobody can predict itThe Moon says that the city will move, but no sky, number or AI can say where each place lands. The city is only ever known as it is now.

Places and IDs

PlaceAny shop, home, stall or pharmacy that the city can move.
Place IDA permanent code such as P-0417. It goes wherever the place goes and never changes.
Door tagA brass tag on a door with raised digits you can read by touch. It shows the place's ID.
Same cell, different IDSeveral places can share one cell. The ID is what tells them apart.
Feel tagA short phrase a person adds to a place: a smell, a sound, a colour or a story.
Find by feelingDescribing a place in your own words so the finder can offer matches. After Calvino's Invisible Cities.
CandidateA place the finder thinks you might mean. It shows up to three.
Strong, partial, weak matchHow many of your words matched the place's feel tags.
Discriminating questionWhen two places match equally, one question that tells them apart.

The Register

RegisterThe city's AI. It knows only the present: where every place is now.
Present onlyThe Register never predicts. It publishes where things are and stamps the time.
BroadcastThe Register's published map, sent out after each shift, about 4:30 to 5:00 am.
FreshThe Register has caught up with the city.
Out of dateAfter an aftershock, the Register still shows the old map for a while.
OfflineNo broadcast at all. The Way Dial can only show the last one, with its age.
TokenOne new place search or one new route from the Register. Everyone gets 8 a day.
WaterThe Register uses water to search, and the Earth only allows so much. That is why tokens are limited.
FreeSaved places on your Way Dial, and health trips, never cost a token.
Health tripA trip for medicine or care. Always free.
Witness slipA note a person sends to correct a place's cell or add a feel tag.

Getting there

Four movesIn, out, clockwise, anticlockwise. Every route is made of them.
InToward the Centre Hill, to a lower ring number.
OutAway from the Centre Hill, to a higher ring number.
ClockwiseAround the ring toward the next notch number.
AnticlockwiseAround the ring toward the previous notch number.
RouteThe list of moves from where you are to where you are going.
WalkAbout 8 minutes per cell. Anyone can do it.
WheelsAbout 3 minutes per cell. Cannot cross an open canal except at the bridge.
Ring tramRuns clockwise on rings 2 and 3 and stops at odd notches. It rides bedrock rails, so it never moves.
Spoke busRuns in and out along notches 1, 3, 5 and 7 on bedrock. It cannot cross an open canal.
Canal ferryCrosses between rings 2 and 3 on notches 7, 8, 1, 2 and 3, only while a canal is open.
Travel modeHow you move. Press the brass button until the mode you want is lit, or tap the mode lamp to hear it.

The Way Dial

Way DialA brass pocket device with no screen. It remembers your places by ID and listens to the Register.
Receive onlyThe Way Dial listens to the Register's broadcast and never sends anything.
Saved placeOne of up to 8 places the dial remembers, by ID, on the device. The copper button steps through them: press twice for the next one.
Printed manualA folded paper manual in a compartment on the back. Open the lid like a TV remote battery lid, slide it out, read it, put it back, close the lid.
Copper button (read)The round copper button. Press: the dial reads the current item aloud. Press twice: next item. Hold: it reads the instructions for the current mode.
Brass button (mode)The ridged square brass button. Each press changes what the dial tells you. Four lamps show the mode by shape you can feel: circle, square, triangle, diamond.
My placesMode 1 (circle). Reads your saved places by ID, with feel and route.
Around meMode 2 (square). Reads what is near: anchors, data centres, the canal, the bridge and nearby places, by ring and notch.
StatusMode 3 (triangle). Reads how fresh the Register is, how many tokens remain, and the centre colour.
How I travelMode 4 (diamond). Reads and changes your travel mode: walk, wheels, ring tram, spoke bus or ferry.
Save a placeHold the dial against a door tag. The ID goes into its memory.
Centre glowThe ring around the middle of the dial. Steady green: Register fresh. Pulsing amber: out of date. Dark: no signal.
Rim of 27 lightsOne light is lit: the Moon's star patch today, 1 to 27. It carries no status.
Steady greenThe Register is fresh.
Pulsing amberThe Register is out of date.
Dark centreNo signal. The dial can only show the last broadcast.
Arrow windowFour arrows: in, out, clockwise, anticlockwise. The lit one is your next move.
You are hereThe dial knows its own cell from beacons in the bedrock. This is an assumption we still need to test.
Backup wheelsTwo small wheels under the body that set your ring and notch by hand if the beacons fail.
SpeakerReads the route and the place aloud.
Solar cellKeeps the dial running without charging.

On the ground

Earth signalsWhat the ground itself tells you: warmth, hum, tremor and dew.
Warm groundHeat in the ground from a fresh shift.
HumThe low sound of the ground. A changed hum means a shift.
TremorShaking you feel right now.
Dew on stoneA fresh dew sheen on stone that is newly exposed.
KeeperA trained neighbour at each anchor with a big Reference Dial and a chalk board.
Reference DialA large copy of the Way Dial kept by a keeper.
Chalk signA handwritten note at a place itself.
Walk by anchorsSteering from one anchor to the next.
Rule of TwoGo when at least two of three sources agree: the Register, the ground, and people.
